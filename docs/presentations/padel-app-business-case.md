## 4. The reality of building an app business

We hear about developers making £50,000 a month, but rarely about those who spent two years on something that made very little. Good engineering is only part of it.

- **Problem:** are we solving a genuine, recurring problem?
- **Demand:** do enough people actively want a solution?
- **Acquisition:** can we reach those people affordably?
- **Retention:** will they repeatedly return?
- **Monetisation:** are some willing to pay?
- **Profitability:** can revenue exceed the cost of running and growing the business?

## 5. Our angle: padel on holiday

Plan and organise matches before you travel, or find a game while you are there.

- **Before you go:** find players and matches at your destination, organise a match ahead of time, arrive with a game lined up.
- **While you are there:** see open matches nearby, ask to join as a visitor, meet local players.
- **Why it is different:** existing apps focus on your local scene or on booking courts. Padel around a holiday is largely unserved. To confirm.
- **Later:** multilingual support, since players on holiday often do not share a language.

Already in the app: browse open matches by destination, request a place, save trips. To validate: check competing apps for trip-based features, and ask travelling players whether the need is real and recurring.

## 6. What holidays change for us

Why it could help:

- Dense where it counts: start in a few holiday destinations (for example Marbella, Málaga, Lisbon).
- A clear need: visitors know nobody locally and have a fixed trip.
- Clubs want visitors: holiday-area clubs can fill quieter courts.
- Revenue to test: traveller premium features, club listings, booking referrals.

What to watch:

- Seasonality.
- Occasional use: a visitor may use the app once or twice a year, so retention needs local players too.
- Two-sided supply: visitors need local hosts.
- Language: English first, more later.

First test: do travelling players plan padel before a trip, and would they use an app to do it?

## 7. Where revenue could come from

Premium player subscriptions. Club and venue subscriptions. Promoted matches and events. Tournament and league management. Court booking commissions. Advertising and sponsorship. Equipment brand partnerships. Coaching discovery and referral fees.

- **Stays free:** finding players, creating matches and joining matches.
- **Possible premium:** advanced statistics, enhanced matchmaking, rankings, advanced search filters, personalisation.

## 11. The biggest challenge: building a community

Cold-start loop: too few players, too few matches, poor first experience, users stop returning, the community stalls.

5,000 users scattered across the UK is still a poor experience. 300 active players around three local clubs means matches every day. **Local density matters more than total downloads.**

## 12. Where the first players come from

Padel club partnerships. QR codes at venues. WhatsApp and Facebook groups. Coach partnerships. Local tournaments and events. Player referral schemes. Social media content. Club ambassadors. Equipment brand partnerships. App Store optimisation.

Clubs and existing communities give direct access to groups of active players. Start there.

## 13. Five phases, each ending in a decision

| Phase | When | Exit test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Validate | Weeks 1–2 | 3 clubs ready to trial and 50 interested players |
| 2 Build the MVP | Weeks 3–10 | 100 genuine users and 20 organised matches |
| 3 Retain | Months 3–4 | Weekly returns, matches organised without us |
| 4 Monetise | Months 4–6 | 20 paying customers or 5 paying clubs |
| 5 Grow | Months 6–12 | Subject to the results of earlier phases |

Timescales are indicative.

## 14. Phases 1 and 2

**Phase 1, market validation:** talk to at least 30 players and 5–10 clubs; learn how players organise matches today; find frustrations with existing apps; explore willingness to pay. Success: 3 clubs willing to trial, 50 players genuinely interested, evidence of a recurring problem.

**Phase 2, MVP:** sign-up, profiles and skill levels; location-based discovery; match creation, discovery and join requests; match management and notifications; basic communication, analytics and feedback. Success: 100 genuine users, at least 20 matches organised, positive feedback. Avoid complex features not needed to validate the core proposition.

## 15. Phases 3 to 5

- **Phase 3, retention:** improve onboarding, learn why people leave, improve matchmaking, add referrals, deepen club ties. Success: players return weekly, matches run without founders, referrals appear.
- **Phase 4, monetisation:** test player memberships, pilot paid club plans, explore sponsorship, learn the cost of a customer. Success: 20 customers or 5 clubs, they keep paying, a route to covering costs.
- **Phase 5, growth:** new areas, more club partnerships, premium features, tournaments and leagues, booking integrations. Only if phases 1 to 4 show the model works.

## 16. Measure matches, not downloads

- **Community:** registered users, weekly and monthly active users, matches created, matches filled, time to fill a match.
- **Loyalty:** repeat match participation, 30-day retention, organic referrals.
- **Money:** paying subscribers and clubs, monthly recurring revenue, customer acquisition cost, cancellation rate.

The key early metric is successful matches organised through the platform.

## 17. Start small, spend as evidence grows

Expo, Supabase and Vercel let us launch an initial product without significant infrastructure spend.

- **Spend early on:** essential infrastructure, App Store and Google Play distribution, user research and product development, customer acquisition and club partnerships, analytics and feedback.
- **Avoid:** complex infrastructure and premature scaling, features nobody has validated.
- **Rough running cost at launch:** about £36 a month ex VAT, plus about £110 in year one for Apple, Google Play and a domain. Estimates, see the infrastructure map.

## 18. Why padel suits a community platform

Inherently social. Players come back again and again. Players need other people to organise games. Skill and availability need matching. Clubs benefit from more participation. Communities drive organic growth. Consumer and business revenue streams. Repeatable in other countries.

Be realistic: competition includes established booking platforms and free WhatsApp groups. Players need a clear reason to choose us.

## 19. Five questions to answer next

1. Can we get 100 people genuinely using the app?
2. Can they organise matches every week?
3. Can we build relationships with local clubs?
4. Will people or businesses pay for the value?
5. Can we repeat this in other areas?

The objective is not just a great app. It is a successful business built around one.
